Sending a tip

Tips are the part of this job that can't be automated, and the part where a small outlet can still beat a large one. A few things make a tip usable:

  • Documents over description. A PDF, a spreadsheet, a photo of a notice does more than a paragraph about one.
  • Say how you know. "I worked there for six years" beats "I heard".
  • Tell us what's checkable. Names, dates, reference numbers, the specific decision.
  • Flag your own risk. Whether your employer knows, and whether you'd go on the record.

We'll confirm receipt within two working days and tell you honestly if we're not going to pursue it. Anonymity is granted by an editor, not assumed — see the anonymous source rules.

What we can't do

  • Give legal, medical or financial advice on a personal situation. We'll point you at the primary guidance, not a verdict.
  • Cover every press release. Volume isn't the goal; we'll say if it's not for us.
  • Chase a story for a PR client without knowing who the client is. Say so up front.
  • Remove a story because it's inconvenient. We correct and update; we rarely unpublish, and we explain when we do.

If we don't reply

Our targets are published above so you can hold us to them. Missed a target by more than three working days, or unhappy with the answer? Escalate to standards@pulseamericanews.com and say what went unanswered. The complaints process sets out the full three stages, ending with the Editor-in-Chief.
