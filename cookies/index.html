01What cookies are

A cookie is a small text file a site asks your browser to keep, so the site can recognise that browser later. Related technologies — local storage, session storage, IndexedDB, service-worker caches — do the same job with different mechanics and no expiry date.

Cookies are neither good nor bad in themselves. They become a privacy question when they identify you across sites, persist for years, or feed an advertising profile you never agreed to. This page covers all of it, not just the ones we set.

02Our position in one line

Pulse America News is static HTML served from a static host. There is no content management system, no server-side session, no ad server and no third-party analytics script. That means the site sets no cookies of its own.

We did not choose this to avoid compliance work — though it helps. It means a reader with JavaScript off, cookies blocked and a tracker blocker running sees the same article as anyone else, at the same speed.

The trade is measurement. We cannot tell you how many people read a piece beyond what our host reports in aggregate. If that changes, section 9 obliges us to say so here and in the version history.

What we do not use

  • No analytics cookies. No Google Analytics, Plausible, Fathom, Matomo or equivalent.
  • No advertising or retargeting. No ad server, no DSP, no Meta pixel, no Google Ads tag.
  • No social embeds that track by default. Where a post is embedded it is a plain blockquote; the network's script only loads if it renders.
  • No identity cookies. No login, so nothing to remember you by.
  • No consent-management platform. Nothing to manage.

04Local storage and similar

Two of our tools remember your last input so you do not have to retype it. This is stored by your browser, on your device, under our origin. It is never transmitted to us and we cannot read it.

Browser storage used by our interactive tools
Key Where Purpose Cleared by
pan.threshold local storage Remembers the project size and state last entered in the threshold calculator. Clearing site data
pan.gate local storage Records that you have already given an address to the threshold-alert box, so the prompt does not reappear. Clearing site data
pan.theme local storage Your light or dark preference, if you have overridden the system setting. Clearing site data

Storage keys are namespaced pan. so you can identify ours at a glance. Because local storage is not a cookie, it survives until you clear it or your browser does — some browsers clear it under "clear browsing data", others under "site data" only.

05Third-party content

Some pages load files from other origins. Images come from our own host, but stock photography is served from images.unsplash.com. Those requests disclose your IP address and user agent to that provider, as any image request does. They do not set cookies unless you go on to visit that provider directly.

Share buttons at the foot of articles are plain links, not scripts. Clicking one hands you to the network's own site, where its cookies and policy apply.

If we add an embedded video, chart or newsletter widget in future, the provider may set cookies when it loads. Any such addition is recorded in section 3 before or at the moment it goes live.

06Consent and banners

Consent is only required for cookies that are not strictly necessary. We set none, so there is no banner to dismiss — nothing is gated, and no cookie wall stands between you and an article.

Two consequences follow. First, there is nothing to opt into, so nothing to opt out of. Second, if we ever add analytics, a banner becomes necessary, and it will be a real one: refuse and the non-essential cookies are not set.

07How to control and delete

You can block or clear everything we use without losing access to any article. Settings live in your browser:

  • Chrome — chrome://settings/cookies
  • Edge — edge://settings/content/cookies
  • Firefox — Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Cookies and Site Data
  • Safari (macOS) — Settings, then Privacy, then Manage Website Data
  • Safari (iOS) — Settings, then Apps, then Safari, then Advanced

Blocking all cookies still leaves every page readable. The only degradation is that the calculator forgets your last entry between visits.

Private or incognito windows discard everything when closed, which is a perfectly reasonable way to read us.

08Do Not Track and GPC

We honour Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track automatically, not because a script reads them but because there is nothing to switch off. Since we sell no data and run no cross-site tracking, an opt-out signal describes the default.

Where an opt-out matters is the newsletter: we do not sell or rent addresses, so a subscriber's exposure is limited to the provider that sends the mail. That provider is named in the Privacy Policy.
