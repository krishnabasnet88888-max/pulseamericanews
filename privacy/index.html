Privacy policy

Written to be read, not to be skimmed past. If a clause here surprises you later, we've written it badly.

Effective 15 November 2026·Version 1.0·Owner: Standards Desk
The short version

No account, no login, no cookie wall. Theme preference is stored in your browser and nowhere else. We run one privacy-friendly analytics tool and Google Tag Manager, both of which see your IP and pages read. The newsletter stores your email address until you unsubscribe. We don't sell data, and we don't run ad-network tracking unless a campaign needs it — and then we say so.

3. What stays on your device

Two things, both stored locally in your browser and never transmitted:

  • pan-theme — whether you chose dark or light. A single value, no expiry, gone when you clear site data.
  • Nothing else. No user ID, no fingerprint, no consent string, no cross-site identifier.

4. Analytics and third parties

We use Cloudflare Web Analytics for traffic measurement. It is cookieless, sets no client-side identifier, and reports aggregate pageviews, referrers and countries. We use Google Tag Manager (container GTM-5Z4RH797) to load measurement tags; GTM itself sets no cookie until a tag inside it does.

Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, which sees your IP when the stylesheet is requested. Some article images are hot-linked from Unsplash, which sees your IP too. Everything else is served from our own domain.

Our site sits behind Cloudflare, which processes requests, terminates TLS and keeps logs for security and caching. Cloudflare is a processor acting on our instructions.

5. Newsletter

Subscribing stores your email address with [MAIL PLATFORM]. We use it to send the daily briefing and nothing else. Every send has a one-click unsubscribe; unsubscribing removes you from the active list, and a suppression record of the address is kept so we don't re-add you by accident.

We do not sell, rent or share the list. Opens and clicks are tracked at aggregate level unless your mail client blocks images, in which case opens simply go uncounted.

6. Tips and sources

Tips get stronger handling than ordinary site data:

  • Tip email and documents are held on our own storage, not a public drive, with access limited to the reporter and editor on the story.
  • They are not passed to third-party tools — including AI tools — without telling you first.
  • They are not shared with other outlets without your agreement.
  • They are kept after publication so a story can be defended, and destroyed or returned on request.

Separately, some tips arrive via Signal, where the conversation is stored on the device and on Signal's servers, not ours. See the tip line for the security trade-offs of each channel.

One honest caveat: a source who later becomes a named subject of a story is, by definition, in the published record. Anonymity in the copy and privacy in the database are different things.

7. Advertising and tracking

We sell display advertising direct. At present our ads are static images or self-hosted HTML5, which means no ad-network cookie, no real-time bidding and no cross-site profile built from your reading here.

When this changes

If a campaign requires an ad server or third-party measurement pixel, that will set cookies and this page will be updated before the campaign runs, with the vendor named. We will not switch to programmatic silently and call it "partners".

Sponsored articles are labelled and count as editorial for analytics purposes. Advertisers see aggregate numbers, never your identity.

8. Legal bases (UK/EU GDPR)

  • Legitimate interests — cookieless analytics, security, and replying to correspondence. Our interest is running and protecting a news site; the data is minimal and no profile is built.
  • Consent — the newsletter. Withdraw it any time by unsubscribing.
  • Contract — delivering the newsletter you asked for.
  • Legal obligation — where a court or regulator compels disclosure.
  • Journalism exemption — source material held for reporting benefits from the journalism derogations, which is why it isn't deleted on a fixed timer.

10. Your rights

Depending on where you live you may have some or all of: access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, portability, and the right to withdraw consent. Exercise any of them at privacy@pulseamericanews.com.

We respond within one month and free of charge. We may ask you to verify the email address concerned. There is one limit worth stating: material that has become part of a published article usually stays in the article, because the published record is the point — but we'll fix what's wrong and tell you what we can't remove.

If you're in the UK or EU and unhappy with our answer, you can complain to your national supervisory authority. If you're in California, you have the rights set out in the CCPA/CPRA as amended, including to know, delete, correct and opt out of sale or sharing — and since we don't sell or share, opt-out is largely academic here. We honour Global Privacy Control signals as an opt-out of sale/sharing where applicable.

11. Cookies

At the time of writing: none set by us. The site works with cookies blocked entirely. A full cookie table lives at Cookies and is updated the same day anything changes. Because there are no non-essential cookies yet, there is no consent banner — a banner over a site that sets nothing is theatre.

12. International transfers

We're based in [COUNTRY]. Our infrastructure providers are US-based, so data reaches the US. Transfers rely on Standard Contractual Clauses or an adequacy decision as applicable to each provider. Aggregate analytics data is not personal data in practice.

13. Children

The site is aimed at a general audience and not at children under 13. Our sex-education section is written for teenagers and older. We don't knowingly collect data from children; the newsletter needs only an email address, which a parent can supply.

14. Changes

Material changes get a new version number, a new effective date, and a note in the log below. We don't do silent edits to a privacy policy.
