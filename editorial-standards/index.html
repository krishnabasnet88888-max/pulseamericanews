Editorial standards

Rules we hold ourselves to, published so they can be checked against the work.

Source hierarchy

First: primary documents — statutes, dockets, orders, filings, datasets, peer-reviewed papers. Second: named practitioners and institutional experts on the record. Third: journalism. A claim resting only on the third tier is labelled as such.

Numbers

Every figure carries attribution. If no source exists, the figure is written as unknown rather than estimated. Arithmetic we perform ourselves is shown so a reader can repeat it.

Unnamed sources

Used only where a named source would be exposed to retaliation, and the reason is stated in the sentence.

Second-hand figures

Where a number comes from an aggregator rather than the primary document, the page says so explicitly.

Corrections

Errors are corrected in place with a dated note at the foot of the article. The process is set out on the corrections page.

Updates

Revised articles carry a visible update line and a changed dateModified.

AI policy

Articles are drafted with AI assistance and edited by a human. Every claim is checked against the source before publication. Where a piece contains no first-hand reporting, it says "we did not test this" in the method box.

The named editor on each piece is the person who verified it. If a claim cannot be verified against a primary source, it is cut rather than hedged.

Diversity and contributors

We commission by beat and by what a piece needs, not by profile. Contributor credit is given for the work done, and staff and freelance rates are the same for the same commission. Where a section depends on a specialist credential — medicine, law, statistics — the holder of that credential reviews the piece and is named in the byline.

Advertising and independence

Display advertising is separated from editorial. Sponsored material is labelled, sits in a distinct visual style, and is excluded from editorial feeds. Advertisers buy audience, not approval: no advertiser sees copy before publication and none has sign-off. Full terms are on the advertise page.

Complaints process

Three stages, and any complaint about accuracy starts at stage one.

  • Stage 1 — the editor on the story. Email corrections@pulseamericanews.com with the URL, the sentence and the source that contradicts it. Target: acknowledgement within one working day.
  • Stage 2 — the standards desk. If stage 1 doesn't settle it, email standards@pulseamericanews.com saying what went unanswered. Target: five working days.
  • Stage 3 — the Editor-in-Chief. The final internal call. Outcomes are published in the corrections log where they change the record.

Every stage is free and open to anyone, not just subjects of a story.
