Rhode Island has the second-highest electric rate in the country — and no data center tariff

Rhode Island's residential rate reached 25.14 cents a kilowatt-hour in August 2026, up 10.4%. Only Massachusetts and Hawaii are higher. The state has no large-load instrument, and 10.4% is the fourth-largest mainland increase.

Rhode Island combines the two worst attributes in the country: the second-highest rate and a 10.4% increase. Both trace to the same place. The state generates almost nothing of its own, imports nearly all of it into a constrained ISO-NE load pocket, and pays a renewable portfolio standard on the bill.

The 10.4% is not the renewable standard. It is capacity — the same ISO-NE clearing price that pushed Massachusetts to 11.9% and Maine to 10.8%. The standard is why the base is already 25 cents.

Why a state with no data centers pays data center prices

Rhode Island hosts almost no data center capacity. It is in a capacity zone priced by a regional auction driven by load in Boston and Connecticut. When that auction clears tenfold higher, Rhode Island pays, and gets no offsetting host-community benefit.

The comparison that makes it concrete: Ireland, at 44.7 cents, built enormous capacity per head and got a higher bill. Rhode Island built none and got the second-highest bill in America. Both are the same failure mode in different directions — the load arrived somewhere, and the price went everywhere.

What would change it

A tariff, and the state has the cheapest possible moment to write one: before the load arrives. Oregon's Schedule 96 applies at 20 megawatts and assigns the full distribution upgrade to the facility. Virginia's GS-5 goes further at 25 megawatts with $1.5 million a megawatt in collateral and a 14-year term. Full terms here.

Rhode Island's advantage over Maine is size: a single 100 MW campus is a measurable share of state load, which makes the negotiating position stronger, not weaker.

What to do if your bill jumped

  • Get the line items. Rhode Island Energy must itemise. Check whether the rise is in the supply line, the standard offer reset, or the fixed charge.
  • Compare to last August. Not last month. The standard offer resets twice a year and the reset dates are what move the number.
  • Comment at the PUC. There is no large-load docket. That is the reason to write.
  • Check efficiency and low-income eligibility. At 25 cents a kilowatt-hour, a heat pump conversion pays back faster here than anywhere else in the country.

Questions

Why did my Rhode Island electric bill go up?

ISO-NE capacity charge, constrained import capacity, and a renewable portfolio standard paid on the bill. Rhode Island generates very little in-state.

How much did Rhode Island electricity rise?

10.4% in the year to August 2026, from 22.77 to 25.14 cents a kilowatt-hour. It is the second-highest rate in the country.

Does Rhode Island have a data center tariff?

No. Rhode Island is one of 28 states with no large-load instrument, despite having the second-highest residential rate in the country.

Is Rhode Island electricity the most expensive in the US?

No — second. Massachusetts is higher at 25.61 cents and Hawaii far higher at 47.18. Rhode Island ranks 49th cheapest of 51 jurisdictions.
