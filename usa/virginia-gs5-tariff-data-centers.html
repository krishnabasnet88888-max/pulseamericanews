Virginia's GS-5 is the strictest data center tariff in the country

Effective January 2027, any data center above 25 megawatts in Virginia posts $1.5 million per megawatt in collateral, signs for 14 years, covers 85% of the transmission demand it creates, and pays an exit fee. A 100 MW campus posts $150 million before it breaks ground.

GS-5 is the most complete large-load instrument any state has passed, and the number to read is the collateral. At $1.5 million a megawatt, a 100 MW campus posts $150 million before a spade goes in the ground. That is not a filing fee. It is a claim on capital, and it changes which projects get built and where.

The 85% transmission figure is the second number. A project does not pay for the transmission it uses — it pays for 85% of the demand it creates, which is the whole point: the upgrade is assigned to the cause rather than socialised across the rate base.

The five elements, and which states have them

Every tariff with teeth contains some combination of five things. Virginia has all five.

Nothing else in the country matches it. Oregon's Schedule 96 at 20 megawatts assigns the full distribution upgrade but is thinner on security. Oklahoma's statute is written in statute and carries residential protection, but no collateral schedule. Ohio's AEP tariff has a 25 MW floor and no collateral. Every state, with citations, is in the tracker.

Did it work? Partly.

Virginia rose 7.9% — high, but below Maryland at 11.6%, Maine at 10.8%, Rhode Island at 10.4% and Pennsylvania at 10.2%, while absorbing a far larger volume of load than any of them. On any per-megawatt basis Virginia is far ahead of the states that rose less.

But it rose, because GS-5 takes effect in January and the auctions that produced this year's bills cleared before it. The honest reading is that Virginia bought itself a better 2028 at the cost of a worse 2026, and that the state that hosts the load should be expected to pay more, not less, in the year the load arrives.

What GS-5 does not do

  • It does not lower a bill. No tariff does. Six states with published figures averaged +7.0% against +7.6% for five PJM states without one.
  • It does not catch the 12 MW facility. 25 megawatts is four times lower than the dead federal bill's 100 MW and still four times higher than Oregon.
  • It does not cover existing campuses. It binds new load only.

Questions

What is Virginia's GS-5 tariff?

A large-load tariff effective January 2027 for data centers above 25 megawatts. It requires $1.5 million per megawatt in collateral, a 14-year minimum term, coverage of 85% of the transmission demand created, and an exit fee.

How much collateral does a 100 MW campus post?

$150 million, before the project breaks ground. The rate is $1.5 million per megawatt at the 25 megawatt floor and above.

Did GS-5 stop Virginia's rate rising?

No. Virginia rose 7.9%. It rose less than Maryland, Maine, Rhode Island and Pennsylvania while absorbing far more load, but it rose.

Why 85% of transmission and not 100%?

A project rarely uses a full upgrade's capacity alone, so 85% is an attribution estimate rather than a subsidy. The remaining 15% stays in the rate base on the theory that other load benefits from the line.
