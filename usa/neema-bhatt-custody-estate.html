What happens to Neema Bhatt: custody, the estate and the slayer rule

The verdict closed a murder case. It opened nothing for the child at the centre of it. Custody, her mother's estate and the death certificate are three separate proceedings, and the missing body sits under all of them.

By Daniel Reyes· 16 November 2026·6 min read

Nothing is settled for Neema. She is about two years old and has been in the care of her maternal grandmother, Gita Kafle, and uncle, Mahesh Kafle, since they arrived from Nepal in October 2024. The conviction does not transfer custody, does not terminate her father's parental rights, and does not open her mother's estate.

Neema Bhatt, born in 2024, has been raised since October that year by her maternal grandmother and uncle. Her father's conviction removes him as a physical custodian but is not itself a custody order. Her mother's estate needs a death certificate, and a death certificate needs a judicial finding.

Custody

Virginia treats custody as a separate question from criminal liability. A murder conviction is powerful evidence in a custody case and it is not a determination of one. Three routes exist and they are not mutually exclusive.

  • De facto arrangement. What exists now. Neema lives with her grandmother and uncle; nobody has formally ordered it.
  • Guardianship or custody petition. The Kafles can ask a juvenile and domestic relations court for an order. A conviction makes this close to unopposed.
  • Termination of parental rights. Slower and separate. It matters for adoption, for permanency and for anything that requires Bhatt to stop being a decision-maker.
The immigration complication

Neema is a US-born child. Her grandmother and uncle are Nepali nationals. Their ability to stay, work and keep her turns on guardianship and on her status as an anchor — not on the verdict. Nothing in the criminal case adjusts an immigration position.

The estate

This is where the missing body does real work. An estate needs a death certificate. Virginia can issue one on a judicial finding, but without remains the finding takes longer, and every step is more contestable because there is no physical fact behind it.

The certificate is the key that turns. Without it there is no personal representative, no authority over accounts, no claim on a policy, no power to deal with the house.

The slayer rule

Normally a surviving spouse inherits. A killer should not. Virginia's slayer statute addresses this: a person who kills the decedent is treated as having predeceased them for inheritance purposes.

Two features matter. It applies despite the will — a named killer still loses. And the standard is not identical to the criminal one, so a conviction helps but does not finish the argument. It takes a separate proceeding, and it takes time the family has already spent two years spending.

The practical effect of a successful slayer claim is that the estate passes as if Bhatt had died first — which, with a child, means it goes to Neema, held in trust or guardianship until she is eighteen. Bhatt would still be her father and still her legal parent.

The survivor benefit

A child's Social Security survivor benefit depends on the deceased parent's earnings record and on the death being established. The certificate is again the gate. It is a monthly payment, not a lump sum, and for a two-year-old it runs to eighteen.

Bhatt's own work authorisation derived from Mamta's employment, which is covered in the immigration explainer.

Common questions

Who is caring for Neema?

Her maternal grandmother Gita Kafle and uncle Mahesh Kafle, both of whom came from Nepal in October 2024.

Does the conviction decide custody?

No. Custody is a separate proceeding. The conviction makes a guardianship order close to unopposed but does not itself grant one.

Can the estate be settled without a body?

Slowly, on a judicial finding of death. The certificate is the gate and it is more contestable without remains.
