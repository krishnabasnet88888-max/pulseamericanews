Data center threshold calculator

The dead federal bill applied at 100 megawatts. Oregon's tariff applies at 20. Virginia's at 25. Enter a size and a state.

What this compares

Two things, and they are not the same thing.

The first is the floor in S. 5028 and HR 9340, the Ratepayer Protection Act: 100 megawatts. That bill passed the House 417-3 and failed a Senate cloture vote 57-43, so the federal floor is not in force — it is a counterfactual, and that is exactly what makes it useful to compare against.

The second is the threshold in each state's active large-load tariff. Oregon's Schedule 96 starts at 20 megawatts. Virginia's GS-5 starts at 25. Both catch facilities the federal bill would have missed by a factor of four or five.

The full tracker carries the docket number for each so you can check. Where a state is not listed here the calculator returns "no published floor" rather than guessing.

How to read the result

A project is only "covered" when the size clears the floor of an instrument that is actually in force. Below 100 megawatts in a state with no tariff, nothing covers it — that is the gap the bill was written about and did not close.

Method

Federal floor from the text of S. 5028. State floors from published tariff sheets and commission orders. No modelling, no interpolation. Last reviewed 15 November 2026.
