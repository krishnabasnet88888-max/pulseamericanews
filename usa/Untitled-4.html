Household bills · Pump prices

Thanksgiving gas hits a record $3.64 — then starts collapsing

The most expensive Thanksgiving tank on record, and the fastest fall in two years, arriving in the same week. Both are true. The gap between North Carolina and Hawaii is now $2.54 a gallon.

By Krishna Basnet24 November 2026, 07:00 ET18 min readData: AAA, EIA, USDA
  • $3.64National average, 23 Nov
  • $3.58Prior Thanksgiving record, 2022
  • $2.54Spread, cheapest to dearest state
  • 72.9mDriving, per AAA

The national average price of regular gasoline stood at $3.6366 a gallon on Monday, according to AAA's daily survey — 2.4 cents above the $3.58 that stands as the all-time Thanksgiving Day record, set in 2022. If the number holds through Thursday it will be the most expensive Thanksgiving at the pump in the ninety-odd years AAA has kept the series. It is, on present arithmetic, already the most expensive.

It is also falling faster than at any point since the spring of 2024, and the two facts are not in conflict. They are the same fact viewed from different ends: a price that ran to $4.48 in May, held above $4.00 through the first week of November, and has since given back 42 cents in six days. Every day that passes, the record gets thinner. Thursday's average could land above $3.58 or below it, and the difference between those outcomes is roughly four more days of the current rate of decline.

That is the honest shape of this holiday. There is no version of it where the pump is cheap. There is a version where it is less expensive than it was on Friday, and a version where the number that gets written down on Thursday is the worst one ever recorded.

What the drive actually costs, by state

National averages hide a spread that decides what Thanksgiving costs a household more than any other variable. The cheapest state, North Carolina, is at $3.06. The most expensive, Hawaii, is at $5.60. That is a difference of $2.54 a gallon — and on a 600-mile round trip in a 26 mpg car, it is $56.53.

Eleven states now sit below $3.35 a gallon. Twenty-two sit at $3.57 or above. The line between them is mostly tax policy and distance from a refinery, and only partly crude: state fuel taxes run from 8.9 cents a gallon in Alaska to 73.6 cents in California, and the federal tax adds 18.4 cents on top of everything. California's price carries its own refining penalty on top of that — the state's gasoline is a boutique blend no other market can absorb, so a single outage in the Los Angeles basin moves the price of the whole state.

The number that matters more

Gasoline is the visible cost of this holiday. It is not the largest one. A household driving 600 miles is spending $84 on fuel at the national average and roughly $58.63 on the dinner itself — and the dinner rose 6.3% in a year, faster than any other line in the meal.

The diesel chain, and why it reached the turkey

Diesel is the price that does not appear on the sign a driver reads. It averaged $5.96 a gallon nationally on 20 November against $3.69 for regular — a gap of $2.27 that is close to a record, and one that runs from $5.26 in North Carolina to $7.86 in California.

Diesel is up 64% year over year, roughly double the 28% rise in regular. Nearly everything on a Thanksgiving table moved by truck: the bird, the potatoes, the canned pumpkin, the cream. The American Farm Bureau Federation's annual survey, released 18 November, put an average dinner for ten at $58.63 — up 6.3% from $55.18 last year, and driven by record turkey prices, with the USDA's wholesale forecast for a frozen whole hen above $1.74 a pound.

One caveat worth stating plainly, because it complicates the tidy version: Farm Bureau economist Bernt Nelson has said diesel likely had little to do with this year's turkey price. The birds eaten on Thursday were placed and priced months before diesel spiralled. The diesel number is real, it is in the freight system, and it will be in next year's bird more than this one's.

Airfares took the same hit, harder

Flying did not escape. Round-trip domestic fares booked through AAA for the holiday period averaged $800, up 17% from last year. Government data put September airfares 22% above September 2025 as carriers absorbed jet fuel and raised bag fees. Cirium had Thanksgiving bookings running 1.6% behind 2025 in late October. Visa's research arm found card spending on holiday-period flights down year over year for every income band except households above $200,000.

AAA still expects 81.5 million people to travel at least 50 miles between Tuesday and the Monday after — a decline of 121,000, or one-tenth of one percent. Three years of post-pandemic records end here, on a rounding error. Of those travelers, 72.9 million will drive, 5.94 million will fly and 2.6 million will take bus, train or cruise.

"After three years of record highs, the number of travelers is slightly lower partly because of higher travel costs this year," AAA Travel vice president Stacey Barber said. "But travelers won't notice much of a difference."

How a price gets to $4.48 and then to $3.64

The run-up began with the war with Iran, which lifted crude and, more sharply, refined product. Gasoline peaked at $4.48 in May. It stayed above $4.00 into the first week of November — $4.06 on the 6th, a level no previous November had ever reached. Diesel went further and stayed longer, because diesel is the product the freight system cannot substitute.

Three things are now pushing the other way at once. Refineries have finished autumn maintenance and are running hard into winter-blend specification, which is cheaper to make. Crude has come off its highs. And demand falls every year in the fortnight before Thanksgiving, because households front-load the trips they can.

What could stop it is the thing that has stopped every recent decline: a supply shock at a single point. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve sits at 39% of capacity, which leaves less cushion than in previous drawdowns. A refinery fire, a hurricane in the Gulf, a new escalation in the Gulf of Hormuz — any one of them reverses four days of decline in an afternoon. The 42-cent fall of the past week is not a trend yet. It is a window.

What to do with the window

Fill on Thursday, not Wednesday

The single highest-value action available is also the simplest: wait. At the current rate of decline the national average is giving up roughly six to seven cents a day. A 12-gallon fill bought Thursday instead of Tuesday is 60 to 84 cents cheaper, and if the fall steepens the saving is larger. The exception is a station with a queue forming; a 20-minute idle burns about a third of a gallon, which eats the difference.

Do not chase the state line past 25 miles

Crossing into a cheaper state only pays if the detour is short. At a 40-cent gap and 26 mpg, break-even is about 12 miles each way. Beyond roughly 25 miles of extra driving, the arbitrage is a hobby, not a saving.

Cut speed before you cut stops

Drag rises with the square of speed. Dropping from 75 mph to 65 typically returns 15% to 20% fuel economy, which on a 600-mile trip is worth more than any app discount. Tire pressure at the door-jamb figure, not the sidewall maximum, is worth another 1% to 2%.

Use the card, not the loyalty points

Fuel-station cards and grocery-linked programs are running 10 to 30 cents off through the holiday in most metros, and warehouse-club pumps are typically 20 to 40 cents under the street. Both beat a points balance, which is worth a fraction of a cent.

How this was calculated

State prices are AAA's published state averages for 20 November 2026, the most recent complete 50-state set. National figures use AAA's daily series, which stood at $3.6366 on 23 November; the prior Thanksgiving Day record of $3.58 is from the same series in 2022. Diesel figures are the 20 November state averages. Trip costs assume 600 miles at 26 mpg — roughly the current light-duty fleet average — and 23.08 gallons. Fill costs assume a 12-gallon tank. No adjustment is made for station-level variation, which typically runs 30 cents either side of a state average.

The dinner figure is the Farm Bureau's 18 November survey, which prices a fixed basket for ten and is comparable year to year but not a measure of any individual household's spend. Airfare figures are AAA's booked round-trip average and the September CPI airfare series.
