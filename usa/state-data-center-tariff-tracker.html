State data center tariff tracker

Two caveats, stated plainly. First, only Virginia and Oregon publish a threshold in a tariff sheet we have read; the other 25 MW figures are the modal large-load threshold in adopted instruments and are flagged as such, not quoted. Second, the count of 23 approved states comes from the Edison Electric Institute's May 2026 tally — we have not independently confirmed every state on it. Docket numbers are the next pass, and each row will carry one by 30 November.

Why the thresholds matter more than the count

A tariff at 100 megawatts catches hyperscalers and lets mid-size colocation through. A tariff at 20 megawatts catches almost everything. Oregon's Schedule 96 requires a facility over 20 megawatts to fund the full cost of the distribution upgrades it triggers. Virginia's GS-5 goes further on security: a 14-year term, 85 percent of contracted transmission demand, and $1.5 million per megawatt in collateral — $75 million on a 50-megawatt project.

The federal bill's floor was 100 megawatts. In Oregon a 30-megawatt facility was covered by the state and uncovered nationally. That gap, not the vote count, is the story.
