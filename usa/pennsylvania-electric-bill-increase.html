Pennsylvania's electric bill rose 10.2% — and the state's own regulator moved first

Pennsylvania's residential rate reached 18.61 cents a kilowatt-hour in August 2026. PJM capacity is the cause. The Public Utility Commission answered with a framework order in April and a 25-megawatt PPL settlement — both of which bind future load, not the bills already issued.

Pennsylvania's 10.2% is the fifth-largest mainland rise and the fastest among the four large PJM states. The mechanism is the one running through Maryland and Ohio: PJM capacity charge, passed through, with the auction clearing roughly ten times higher between mid-2024 and mid-2025 and the market monitor naming data centers as the primary cause.

What distinguishes Pennsylvania is that its regulator did not wait for the federal bill to fail. The PUC issued a framework order in April 2026 and a 25-megawatt large-load settlement covering PPL territory. That is faster than Maryland, which has an instrument but no published threshold, and faster than Delaware, Michigan, Illinois or New Jersey, which have none.

Did the framework work?

Not yet, and the page will say so rather than pretend otherwise. Pennsylvania rose 10.2%. The six states with a published figure averaged +7.0%. Five PJM states without one averaged +7.6%. Pennsylvania sits inside that spread at the high end.

The reason is the same in every state: a threshold set in April does not reprice an auction already cleared for 2027/28. The capacity charge is embedded in supply rates through mid-2029. What the framework buys is the next auction and the ones after.

Why the politics moved

Pennsylvania's Senate majority flipped on 3 November. Electricity was not the only issue in that result and it was the one with an address: the state's data center build is concentrated in a handful of counties where the bill increase is largest and most legible.

That is the difference between Pennsylvania and Ohio. Ohio wrote its tariff in statute and its politics did not move. Pennsylvania's politics moved first and the instrument is still a settlement rather than a statute — which makes it easier to withdraw.

What to do if your bill jumped

  • Get the line items. A rise in the capacity or supply line is a market event. A rise in riders is a policy decision. A rise in the fixed charge is a rate case.
  • Compare to last August. The relevant comparison is the same month a year earlier, which is how the national table is built.
  • File in the PUC docket. The framework order is open to comment. The tracker carries the citation.
  • Watch the fixed charge. Several states approved a flat rate with a doubled customer charge this year. That is a rate increase wearing a costume.

Questions

Why did my Pennsylvania electric bill go up?

PJM capacity charge passed through by your utility. The 2025/26 and 2026/27 auctions cleared roughly ten times higher than mid-2024 and PJM's market monitor named data centers the primary cause.

What is the PPL settlement?

A 2026 settlement setting a 25-megawatt large-load threshold in PPL territory, alongside a PUC framework order of April 2026. It binds new load, so it does not reprice bills already issued.

How much did Pennsylvania electricity rise?

10.2% in the year to August 2026, from 16.89 to 18.61 cents a kilowatt-hour — fifth-largest in the mainland behind Maryland, Massachusetts, Maine and Rhode Island.

Is 25 megawatts a strict threshold?

Stricter than the dead federal bill, which was 100 megawatts. Looser than Oregon's 20. It catches hyperscalers and mid-size colocation and misses smaller facilities.
