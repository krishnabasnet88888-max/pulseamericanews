# Who pays for data center electricity? Congress answered 417-3, then the Senate killed it

*By Daniel Reyes · 14 November 2026 · Updated 15 November 2026*

Data center electricity is paid for by whoever the regulator says pays — and right now 23 states say the developer does while Congress says nobody. The House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act 417-3 on 16 September. The Senate killed it 57-43 on 30 September. Neither vote changed a single household bill.

## The vote

HR 9340 was introduced in the House by Energy and Commerce chair Brett Guthrie of Kentucky and passed 417-3 on 16 September. S. 5028 was introduced in the Senate by Jon Husted, an Ohio Republican, on 16 July, with 16 cosponsors.

Husted asked for unanimous consent the next day; Martin Heinrich of New Mexico objected. On 30 September a cloture motion failed 57-43, three votes short of sixty. Forty-three Democrats voted no; four crossed.

A bill that got 99.3 percent of the House died on a three-vote margin in the Senate.

## What the bill covered

| Provision | S. 5028 / HR 9340 | State tariffs adopted |
|---|---|---|
| Load threshold | 100 MW | 20–25 MW (Virginia 25, Oregon 20) |
| Duty on the regulator | to "consider" | binding once adopted |
| Who pays grid upgrades | unassigned | the developer |
| Collateral posted | none | $1.5m per megawatt (Virginia) |
| Minimum contract term | none | 14 years (Virginia) |

The federal floor was four to five times higher than the state rules it was written to nudge.

## What Virginia makes developers post

| Project size | Collateral at $1.5m/MW | Minimum term | Transmission demand covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 MW | $37.5 million | 14 years | 85% |
| 50 MW | $75 million | 14 years | 85% |
| 99 MW | $148.5 million | 14 years | 85% |
| 100 MW | $150 million | 14 years | 85% |

At 99 megawatts a developer posts $148.5 million and signs for 14 years — but the federal bill would not have applied.

Oregon's Schedule 96 requires any data centre over 20 megawatts to cover the full cost of distribution upgrades.

## The money already on bills

PJM Interconnection serves more than 65 million people. Its capacity auction price rose roughly tenfold between mid-2024 and mid-2025. Monitoring Analytics, PJM's independent market monitor, called data centres "the primary reason" and puts the two-year cost to PJM's 67 million ratepayers at about $29 billion.

Ratepayers face an extra $7.3 billion in capacity charges this delivery year and $6.5 billion the next. The pass-through reached $16 to $18 a month in Ohio and western Maryland, 63 percent attributed to data centres.

## The pledge is not a mandate

Seven companies signed the March Ratepayer Protection Pledge: Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle and xAI. It is voluntary and unenforced.

> "Compliance is not being monitored or enforced by anyone." — Ari Peskoe, Electricity Law Initiative, Harvard

## The counterargument

EPRI estimated data centres *lowered* average retail rates 6 percent between 2015 and 2024 in average-growth areas. LBNL and Brattle found prices rose 29 percent from 2019 to 2025 but 3 percent inflation-adjusted. Nationally the average effect is small. The rebuttal is distributional, not directional — and PJM is the documented exception.

## Questions

**Did the Senate kill it?** Yes, for this Congress. Cloture failed 57-43 on 30 September 2026.

**How big before the federal bill applied?** 100 megawatts. State tariffs start at 20 to 25.

**Is the pledge binding?** No.

**Is there a stricter bill?** The GRID Act, Hawley and Blumenthal, February 2026. Still in committee.
