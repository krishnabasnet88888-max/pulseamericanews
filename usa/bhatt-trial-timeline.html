Naresh Bhatt trial timeline: from the July 2024 disappearance to the verdict

Twenty-eight months separate a missed shift at a Manassas hospital from a guilty verdict in a Virginia courtroom. This is the dated sequence, with the gaps marked.

By Daniel Reyes· Manassas, Virginia· 16 November 2026·7 min read

Key dates

Disappearance
31 July 2024
First charge
22 August 2024, concealing a dead body
Jury selection
5 October 2026
Verdict
10 November 2026, guilty on all three counts
Judge
Kimberly Irving
Sentencing
12 March 2027

Mamta Kafle Bhatt, 28, disappeared from her Manassas Park home on 31 July 2024. Naresh Bhatt, 39, was charged on 22 August 2024 with concealing a dead body, later indicted for murder. Jury selection began 5 October 2026; a jury convicted him on all counts on 10 November 2026. Sentencing is 12 March 2027.

Two things shape this timeline. The first is that her body was never recovered, so almost every early date rests on circumstantial reconstruction rather than a forensic anchor. The second is that the case ran two years and three months from disappearance to verdict — long enough that the trial dates fall in a different news year from the disappearance.

Dates below are sourced to court record and contemporaneous reporting. Where a date is approximate, it is marked.

2024

  1. 30 July 2024 · approx. 4:30 a.m.

    Naresh Bhatt's phone performs two searches: "diagram of brain" and "chicken farm near me." The timestamps were later put before the jury.

  2. 31 July 2024

    Mamta Kafle Bhatt is last seen. Bhatt later tells police he saw her that morning and heard her getting ready for work on 1 August.

  3. Early August 2024

    She does not appear for shifts at UVA Health Prince William Medical Center. Colleagues — not family, who are in Nepal — raise the alarm. A missing-person report follows.

  4. 14 August 2024

    Friends and family gather outside Manassas Park Police Department. Delegate Danica Roem attends. Volunteers begin organised searches.

  5. 22 August 2024

    Bhatt is taken from his home in handcuffs and charged with a single count of concealing a dead body, a Class 6 felony. The murder charge follows later.

  6. October 2024

    Mamta's mother, Gita Kafle, and brother, Mahesh, arrive from Nepal. Their granddaughter Neema, then about a year old, goes into their care.

2025

  1. March 2025 · approx.

    Nearly eight months after the disappearance, Manassas Park Police Chief Mario Lugo confirms the search has narrowed to a landfill in Luray, Virginia — the Battle Creek facility in Page County. He says publicly that her remains may never be found.

  2. Late 2025

    Pre-trial litigation. Bhatt declines a plea agreement and maintains a not-guilty plea.

2026

  1. 5 October 2026

    Jury selection opens before Judge Kimberly Irving. About 175 candidates are summoned. Bhatt asks for a further delay; it is denied. He pleads not guilty to murder, concealing a dead body and defiling a dead body.

  2. 7 October 2026

    Shortly before noon, a 17-member panel — 12 jurors and five alternates — is seated after two and a half days of selection. That afternoon, prosecutor Matthew Sweet opens for the Commonwealth; defence attorney Tracey Lenox follows.

  3. October 2026

    More than 17 days of testimony. Bhatt takes the stand in his own defence and concedes the dismemberment while insisting the death was a suicide. A police witness tells jurors a recovery is unlikely.

  4. 10 November 2026

    Closing arguments. Roughly four hours later, at about 5 p.m., the jury returns guilty verdicts on all three counts. Bond is revoked and Bhatt is remanded.

  5. 16 November 2026

    Sentencing is fixed for 1 p.m. on 12 March 2027 at Prince William County Circuit Court.

Why the gaps matter

The 26 months between the charge and the trial are not dead time. They contain the landfill operation, the death-certificate question and the whole pre-trial fight — and they are the reason a family in Nepal spent two Christmases without an answer.

What the timeline does not contain

There is no entry for a recovery. That absence is the case. The state proved a killing without producing the thing killed, using the corroboration rule set out in our explainer on no-body convictions.

The verdict itself is covered in the report on the conviction. What happens to the child is in a separate piece, and the sentence arithmetic is in this analysis.
