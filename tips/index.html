Tip line

Send us a tip

Pick the channel that matches how much trouble you'd be in if your name ended up attached to this. The riskier the story, the further down this page you should go.

We'd rather receive a tip badly packaged than not at all. If the instructions below are too much, send an email to tips@pulseamericanews.com and we'll work the rest out with you.

Be honest about this first

Who could be annoyed that you talked? An employer, a ministry, a contractor, a court. If the answer is "someone who can fire or prosecute me", don't use your work email or your work laptop.

Choose a channel

EmailLow risk

tips@pulseamericanews.com

Fine for most things. Use a personal address, not the one your employer administers, and not the one connected to your social accounts.

Known weakness: ordinary email is not end-to-end encrypted and sits on servers at both ends. Don't use it for anything that would hurt to hand over.

SignalMedium risk

[SIGNAL NUMBER, with country code] — message only, say what it's about, no documents first

End-to-end encrypted, and the fastest way to have an actual conversation. Good for documents up to a point.

Known weakness: your phone number is visible to us, and message history lives on your device. If your phone is seized unlocked, so is the conversation. Set disappearing messages if that matters.

Encrypted email (PGP)Medium–high risk

Key: pulseamericanews.com/pgp · fingerprint [40-CHARACTER FINGERPRINT]

Encrypt to our public key and only we can read it in transit. Verify the fingerprint on this page against the one your mail client shows before you trust it.

Known weakness: metadata — subject lines, timestamps, the fact you wrote at all — stays readable. Encryption protects the contents, not the existence of the message.

PostHigh risk

[POSTAL ADDRESS] — mark the envelope "Editorial"

For paper, or for removable media you don't want near a network. Send copies, never originals, and post from a box away from your home or office.

Known weakness: postmarks, handwriting and return addresses. Envelopes get opened and photographed by more parties than you'd expect.

What we don't have yet

No SecureDrop, no air-gapped machine, no key-signing ceremony. We're a small desk. If your material would justify a SecureDrop, tell us and we'll say plainly whether we can handle it or whether you should go to an outlet that can. Overclaiming security is the standard failure of tip pages.

Before you send anything

Strip the fingerprints

  • Office files carry your name. In Word, Excel or PowerPoint: File → Info → Check for Issues → Inspect Document → remove author and comments. Or export to PDF and check the metadata.
  • PDFs keep the author field. Open properties and clear it, or print to a fresh PDF.
  • Photos carry GPS and device models. Strip EXIF if the location matters — but send an unstripped copy too if the location is the story.
  • Screenshots carry your taskbar, tabs and account name. Crop before sending.
  • Scan, don't photograph, where you can — a photo shows your desk, your hands and your timezone.

What makes a tip usable

  • The document itself. A contract, a spreadsheet, a memo, a notice, a photo of a signed page.
  • How you know. "I processed these invoices for three years" is worth more than "this is definitely happening".
  • What can be checked independently. Names, dates, reference numbers, the specific decision and who signed it.
  • Whether it's already public anywhere, even in a buried filing.
  • Your risk, and what you'd want. On the record, background only, or fully anonymous.

What happens next

  1. We confirm receipt within two working days. If we can't, we'll say so rather than leave you guessing.
  2. An editor, not an algorithm, decides whether to pursue it — and tells you either way, including when the answer is no.
  3. If we pursue it, expect questions. Good stories get checked hard, and that includes checking you.
  4. Before publication we agree with you what's attributed and what isn't. Anonymity is granted by an editor, not assumed by the sender — see the anonymous source rules.
  5. You see the passages that quote you or rely on your material before they run, for accuracy only. You don't get approval of the piece.

What we do with your documents

  • Held on our own storage, not a public drive. Access limited to the reporter and the editor on the story.
  • Not uploaded to third-party services without telling you — that includes AI tools, which is worth asking about.
  • Never handed to another outlet without your agreement. Co-publishing is agreed in advance, named.
  • Retained after publication so the story can be defended. Tell us if you want the material returned or destroyed and we'll do it.
  • Originals kept where possible. We tell you if we've had to redact something.

What we can't promise

  • Not being found out. We can protect your name in print. We can't control what your employer infers from a detail only six people knew.
  • Legal shield. Shield protections differ by jurisdiction; we're not a law firm and can't indemnify you.
  • That it runs. Most tips don't become stories. That's not a judgement on the tip.
  • Indefinite secrecy if a court compels disclosure. We'll tell you if that happens.
  • Perfect channel security. Every option above has the weakness listed next to it. Pick the one whose weakness you can live with.

If you're inside an organisation

A short, practical version: don't use the work laptop if you can avoid it, and don't use work Wi-Fi for the first contact. Download documents to a personal device rather than forwarding them through work mail — forwarding leaves a server-side trace. Print at home if printing is safe, and take the pages with you. If your employer runs device management on your phone, assume Signal is visible to them and use the web version on a personal machine instead.
