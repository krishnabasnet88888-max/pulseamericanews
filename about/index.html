About us

Who we are, who pays for this, and how we get it right

Pulse America News is an independent newsroom covering US and world affairs, climate, technology, health and sport. This page is our masthead, our funding disclosure and our rulebook — in one place, so you can judge us on it.

Published by the Editorial Standards Desk· Reviewed by [EDITOR-IN-CHIEF NAME], Editor-in-Chief· Updated 15 November 2026

The short version

  • Independence: editorially independent, owned by [OWNER / ENTITY], with no political party or government funding.
  • Funding: [FUNDING MODEL — subscriptions, newsletter sponsorship, display advertising]. Advertisers never see copy before publication.
  • Who writes: every byline is a real, named person with a published bio and a contact route. No anonymous staff bylines.
  • Sourcing: we name sources and link to primary documents — filings, datasets, court records, peer-reviewed studies. If we can't source a number, we say so.
  • AI: models help us draft and structure; a human editor verifies every published claim. AI-generated images are labelled.
  • Corrections: we fix errors fast, in public, with a dated note. Email corrections@pulseamericanews.com.

What we're for

Most news is written for people with unlimited time and a prior opinion. We write for the reader who has neither. Our beat is the story behind the headline — the arithmetic, the mechanism, the part of the decision that was made before anyone held a press conference.

That means three commitments, and they cost us something. We explain the numbers rather than quoting them at you. We tell you what would have to be true for a claim to hold. And we publish what a story leaves out, including when the omission flatters the people we're covering.

We are new. The site launched in [LAUNCH MONTH YEAR]. We'd rather show you a short, verifiable record than a long, impressive-sounding one, so our editorial standards and corrections log are public from day one.

How we report

  • Primary sources first. Statutes, dockets, agency releases, BLS and Census tables, WHO and UN datasets, peer-reviewed papers. A press release is a tip, not a source.
  • Named sources by default. Anonymity is a concession we explain in the copy, along with what the source's motive is.
  • Numbers carry a link. Every statistic points at the document it came from. If a figure is an estimate, the range and the estimator are both stated.
  • Two-source rule for contested claims. One source is enough only when it's a primary document.
  • Right of reply before publication for any piece making a criticism of a named person or institution, with a reasonable deadline given.
  • No embargo trading. We don't swap coverage for access, and we don't hand copy to subjects for approval — only for fact-checking.
Our AI policy We use language models the way a newsroom uses a researcher: to draft structure, summarise long documents and draft headlines. A human editor is accountable for every published sentence, and any figure a model produces is checked against the primary source before it goes live. Corrections and updates

We get things wrong. What matters is the speed and the visibility of the fix.

  • Factual errors are corrected on the page, with a dated note at the foot saying what changed and when.
  • Material changes to a live story are logged at the bottom of it — not made silently.
  • Headline and summary fixes that don't change the substance are not logged, but the "updated" timestamp moves.
  • We correct on the record even when the error was small and nobody noticed.

Report an error: corrections@pulseamericanews.com, subject line "Correction". Include the URL and the specific claim. We aim to acknowledge within one working day. The running log lives at Corrections.

Images and data

Original photography and graphics are credited to the photographer or to Pulse America News. Licensed stock is credited in the caption. AI-generated illustrations are captioned as such so you always know what was in front of a lens. Data visualisations state their source and date range, and the underlying table is downloadable where licensing allows.

Advertising and independence

Commercial content is labelled "Sponsored" and sits in a different visual style from editorial. Advertisers buy audience, not approval; no advertiser has sign-off on editorial copy. Newsletter sponsorship is disclosed at the top of the send.
